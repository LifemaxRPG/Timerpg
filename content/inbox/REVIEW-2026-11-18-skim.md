# Skim of 320 time-travel drafts, 2026-11-18

All 80 ChatGPT runs on the `content-inbox` branch (2026-11-17 10:57 to 2026-11-18 08:16, prompt v1, 320 scenarios). The first 120 were reviewed in full yesterday (`REVIEW-2026-11-17.md`); its 27 keeps are left out here so nothing is picked twice. This pass read the pitch, hook and why-fun of all 320 and the roles, steps and present day of the candidates. It ignores the wording (every one of the 320 is written in legal paperwork language: on average 37 hedging words such as actual, lawful, genuine, qualified, documented per scenario) and judges only the premise: who, when, what the player does, and what changes on the street.

**Result:** 33 premises kept (best first), with 13 near-duplicates grouped under them (46 drafts used). 247 rejected, plus the 27 already kept yesterday. Quality falls off sharply over the night: from about 21:00 on the 17th most drafts are 'help someone pay a fee, and decades later someone loses a small convenience'. Only 7 of the 33 keeps come from those last 45 runs.

Ratings are out of 5 for the premise after a rewrite, not for the draft as written. None is usable as written: every one needs the text redone in the v2 voice and its invented effect keys replaced by the v2 list.

## Kept premises

### 1. The Money Meant for Leaving (4.5/5)

`2026-11-17-1227`

**Premise:** A sharp young woman has saved her bus fare out of town; steal it, and she stays, takes a job at the bakery, and decades later owns the best bakery on Main Street without ever knowing who robbed her.

**Why it's good:** A cruel act with a warm result: the player does something rotten and comes back to a beloved shop and a woman who thinks staying was fate. Morally sticky, easy to explain, visible on Main Street.

**Needs rewriting:** Drop the night-noise annex twist. Let worst be the bitter version (she stays and resents the town all her life, the bakery is famous and joyless). Make the fail interesting (she borrows the fare and leaves anyway, poorer). Add a clipping of the city career she never had as the twist. Uses the real 'take' verb, not 'proposed:take'.

### 2. The Book That Skipped Its Author's Youth (4.5/5)

`2026-11-17-1426`

**Premise:** Hand a young dreamer the novel they will publish at sixty; they write it at twenty instead, become the town's famous author, and the book still describes the shops and people of the town you came from.

**Why it's good:** The best uncanny idea in the set: a time loop the player can hold. The present has a famous author, a bookshop window and a book that describes the player's original town, which the new town reads as fiction.

**Needs rewriting:** Cut the 'quotation panel' and provenance talk. Express it as tell/place (leave the book on their desk). Stakes: the author's fame changes the town (tourists, a statue, a festival); worst: the author is haunted that they never wrote anything of their own. Uncanny line: 'Funny thing, the book named a dress shop we never had.'

**Near-duplicates grouped here:**

- `2026-11-17-1914` The Voice That Asked for a Private Room: Play someone a recording of their own old voice; weaker, same loop.

### 3. The Missing Name That Later Kept the Search List (4.5/5)

`2026-11-17-2347`

**Premise:** A teenager went missing in the woods one autumn and was found too late; tell the search party where to look, and she comes home, grows up, and her family, her shop and her children are in the present.

**Why it's good:** A clean rescue with the heaviest payoff: a person alive who was a name on a memorial bench. Strong clues from people (the mother, the searchers), an easy player action (tell), and many ripples.

**Needs rewriting:** Throw out the 'search roster retired by a funded plan' ending. Ripples: she marries, opens a shop, her kids exist; the memorial bench is gone. Twist: the boy who was blamed for her disappearance in the old timeline is now unknown and unremarkable, or the searcher who 'found' her becomes a local hero on the player's tip. Uses warn/tell.

### 4. The Coin That Lost Its Miracle (4/5)

`2026-11-17-1712`

**Premise:** Give a coin collector a quarter minted in the present year; the 'coin from the future' becomes the town's famous mystery, drawing crowds and believers, until the calendar catches up with it.

**Why it's good:** Funny, uncanny, and very much this game: a joke object grows into a landmark, a museum and a local legend, and the player watches its meaning run out as the stamped year arrives.

**Needs rewriting:** Remove the custody/label rules. Stakes: the collector gets rich on the curiosity, opens a museum or a roadside attraction on Main Street, a rival calls it a fraud. Express as place(put). Worst: the collector is ruined as a hoaxer. The fun last line: an old-timer turning the coin over in the present year.

### 5. The Postcard from the Lake That Was Not Built (4/5)

`2026-11-17-2017`

**Premise:** In your present a reservoir drowned the old lower town; show the planning clerk a postcard of the lake from the future, the dam is never built, and the drowned street is still standing when you return.

**Why it's good:** Town-scale stakes from one small uncanny act: a whole neighborhood, its houses and families exist or don't. Invert the draft (it ends on a watering calendar) so the payoff is the saved street.

**Needs rewriting:** Flip the direction: the lake exists in the original present and drowned homes. Present: houses, a church and a shop where the water was; worst: the town runs short of water and its biggest employer leaves. ## Rejected

247 rejected (of the 293 not already kept yesterday). The three most common reasons, most rejects having more than one:

1. **Tiny payoff**: the present-day change is one person losing a small habit or amenity (a locker, a parking space, a free ride, a seat at the hall, a reserved hour), or a line on a bill. Nothing the town would gossip about.
2. **Paperwork as the player action**: the player helps someone pay a fee, file an application, finish a course or get a licence. 103 of the 320 pitches start with 'Help/Pay/Fund someone...', almost all of them this.
3. **Nothing to walk to**: the change lives only in archives, registers, contracts and policies (a corrected record, an exemption deadline, a voting right in an association), often resting on props or business kinds the game doesn't have (golf club, motel, tattoo shop, sugarhouse).

Typical rejects:

- `2026-11-17-2246` **The Locker That Stopped Belonging to One Member**: Invest in a gym's locker plan; decades later one member loses his reserved locker. Tiny payoff.
- `2026-11-17-2347` **The Ambulance Subscription That Became a Tax Bill**: Help someone pay a subscription; it ends as a non-optional levy. Paperwork in, paperwork out.
- `2026-11-17-2232` **The Number That Cost More to Leave Out**: A phone-directory listing that later costs a fee to remove. Nothing to see or hear.
- `2026-11-18-0601` **The Passport That Did Not Reserve a Tax Exemption**: The whole story is a missed tax-exemption deadline.
- `2026-11-17-2331` **The Kitchen Paid for by a Life You Ended**: Kill a banker so his heir funds a soup kitchen: a duplicate of the kept 'Empty Chair' and 'Job Left Empty', with no reason for the player to want it.
