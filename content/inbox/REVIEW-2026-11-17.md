# Review of ChatGPT's content, 2026-11-17

Everything ChatGPT committed up to 18:18 (UTC-5): the 8 content batches in `content/inbox/2026-11-17/` (commit 33cd8ca)
and the 30 time-travel runs on the `content-inbox` branch (120 scenarios, prompt v1). Every item was read in full;
per-item verdicts and scores are in `REVIEW-2026-11-17.json`.

## Time-travel scenarios (120)

Format: all 30 files parse, every field present, ids and titles unique, no near-duplicate premises, headlines <= 70
characters, every batch keeps at least half to the six existing verbs (91 of 120 use only them).

Verdicts: 27 keep with the text rewritten, 55 rework, 38 reject; none usable as written.
Mean scores (1-5): fun 2.2, stakes 2.0, prose 1.6, mechanics 2.4.

What went wrong (all measured across the 120):
- The Gazette and the NPCs talk like legal paperwork ("Eligible candidate {A} won the actual contested mayoral
  election. Its recorded result followed the lawful voting process."). The run drifted: the hedging words (actual,
  lawful, eligible, permitted, approved, funded, verified, recorded, documented, licensed) per scenario went from
  1 in the first run to 17 in the last.
- Small stakes: most payoffs are one amenity (a rehearsal hall, a reading room, a toy library), and the twist is mostly
  "an improvement costs someone a small informal amenity" or "someone pays a tenth of their savings". The owner's brief
  (give a poor kid money, come back to a transformed town) is almost never met.
- One template: 1 intervention, 3 steps at about 1-3, 12-14 and 25-30 years, chances 0.8/0.65/0.6, fails 0.2 with no
  effects; exactly 1 clue (always a document, never a person) and 1 NPC line (half bitter, no contractions) each.
- 218 effect keys, most invented and used once (establish_service, qualify, profession, night_noise...); the first
  new_rules_needed entry is the same engine boilerplate in every scenario; many need props the simulation can't make
  ("the withdrawn advertisement", "an adjacent handball wall").

The best ideas (keep, with the text rewritten):
- `scenario-20261117-6722` **Bread Behind the Vault Door**: Sabotaging the bank to get a bakery with the vault door kept as a cupboard is a visible, funny Main Street change in exactly the spirit the owner asked for. The twist should hit harder (savings lost, a mortgage called in), not just 'bank jobs'.
- `scenario-20261117-7621` **The Empty Chair Becomes a Statue**: Killing the mayor creates a dynasty and a statue: sharp irony, with a visible portrait and statue and a strong line. The player is never given a reason to want this high-reputation mayor gone ('political hold' is never shown), and the clue is flat.
- `scenario-20261117-6724` **The Boot That Founded Nothing**: A planted fake relic that grows into a monument is funny and uncanny, and it has the best NPC line ('People still give directions by it'). Step 3 removes the monument 75% of the time, so the usual result is an empty plinth; the monument should more often stand.
- `scenario-20261117-8971` **The Voice Before the Water**: The best hook in the slice: names disappear from a flood memorial because a poor teenager got his radio. The twist (the operator pays 20% of his savings) is weak and should cost something real. Text like 'paid a documented emergency operating contribution' needs a full rewrite.
- `scenario-20261117-8064` **The Ring Carried Back**: The idea that uses time travel best: carry a ring from the present-day lost-and-found back in time, a separation never happens, a person exists who did not, and the drawer is empty when you return. The return verb and its cross-timeline object rule are worth building. Replace 'house_scenario_child' with the existing build/move_in and rewrite the text.
- `scenario-20261117-8224` **The Fair Took the Wrong Turn**: The best small-cause, big-effect chain in the set: turn one arrow, and decades later a covered market hall stands on an empty lot and the fair lives on Main Street. The text is dry ('Organizer {A} obtained emergency permission to hold the arriving fair group'), and the category 'uncanny' is wrong.
- `scenario-20261117-8524` **The Numbers Before the Drawing**: Classic Back to the Future fun: tell a poor, ambitious kid the winning number, then return to a department store and a closed bakery. Requiring an 'unclaimed' prize throws away the obvious twist (the real winner never wins). The uncanny line is good.
- `scenario-20261117-6721` **The Apprentice Who Outgrew the Shop**: A clean monkey's paw: your investment trains the pupil who later closes the teacher's store, with one of the best lines in the set. The stakes are a single storefront swap, and the headlines are dry.
- `scenario-20261117-10472` **The Wedding That Made a Different Family**: The Back to the Future core: break an engagement, and one child never exists while another grows up to open a barber shop on Main Street. Standard levers (no_child, marry, child, found_business) and a discoverable Gazette engagement notice. But present_day forbids any emotional echo ('no ghost, empty bed or grieving memory'), and the headline 'Engaged Couple Ends Its Plans after an Honest Reassessment' is dead on the page.
- `scenario-20261117-6871` **The Tuition Receipt**: Pay for med school, get a town doctor, and someone who died in your timeline answers the door: this is the owner's fantasy almost word for word. The twist ('consuming a quarter of the doctor's personal money') is no twist, and the clue gives the whole answer away.
- `scenario-20261117-8222` **The Clock That Kept the Square Awake**: A stopped town clock that runs and chimes again is visible and audible when you return, suits the clock tower the renderer already has, and the twist (the neighbors lose sleep) is gently funny. Role B is defined but never used in an effect.
- `scenario-20261117-9131` **The Stone in the Schoolbag**: A coherent chain (a rock-mad poor kid becomes a geologist, condemns the quarry, it becomes a park) with a place you can walk into and a good bitter line from the owner. It would be much stronger if the baseline had a collapse that the survey prevents, instead of 'do not require an accident'.
- `scenario-20261117-9134` **The Last Ride After Closing**: Night worker meets garage owner, then a late ride, a bus co-op and a terminal on Main Street: believable steps and a place you can walk into. It also has the best NPC line in the slice. The 'lost free parking' twist is thin.
- `scenario-20261117-9262` **The Lure That Bought the Shore**: The clearest monkey's paw and the closest to the owner's money brief: a bait-shop invention makes its owner rich enough to buy the shore and shut out the poor man who fished there. Strong NPC line. The copy must lose phrases like 'lawfully ended the revocable free-fishing permission'.
- `scenario-20261117-7012` **The Pocket Orchard**: A small gift to a child grows into an orchard you can watch mature over several visits, which is gentle and visible. The twist (no more room for kites) is thin, and the headlines are bureaucratic ('Receives a Public-Use Agreement').
- `scenario-20261117-7172` **The Freezer Route**: A freezer investment that grows into a delivery business and closes the butcher is a believable chain, with a good line ('They do not ask how my week has been'). The prose is jargon ('segregated loads', 'licensed cold room').
- `scenario-20261117-7473` **The Person Named as Guardian**: An orphan who stays in town and opens a parlor with the family recipe is lovely and visible. The text is legalistic ('a legally retained family recipe', 'conditional care plan').
- `scenario-20261117-7773` **The Map Drawn on a Napkin**: One of the few with town-level stakes: a nobody becomes mayor, a Main Street block goes car-free and a clothing store closes, built mostly from existing levers (introduce, run_for_mayor, win_election). Every line of text needs redoing, for example 'secured lawful approval for a funded pedestrian-block plan'.
- `scenario-20261117-7914` **The Haunted Lease**: The best cruel-act premise in the set: you invent a ghost, then come back to find tickets for it sold in the window of the shop you ruined. The text keeps debunking itself ('An exhibition included the inspection report explaining the original pipe noises'). Let the story grow and stay ambiguous.
- `scenario-20261117-8374` **The Tree at the Wrong Corner**: Planting a sapling and returning to a landmark tree is a lovely, visible time-travel payoff, with a mild twist (the roots crack the neighbor's foundation). Simplify the overbuilt setup (child's sketch, adult steward, permission) and rewrite the text.
- `scenario-20261117-9871` **Two Lunchboxes on the Campaign Table**: Two lonely kids introduced at lunch end up rivals for mayor, with their old photo hung beside the winner's portrait. It's one of the few scenarios that changes who runs the town, and it mostly uses standard levers. But the Gazette text is boilerplate ('Subsequent meetings followed their actual choices'), and the 14-year gap between filing to run (step 2) and winning (step 3) makes no sense.
- `scenario-20261117-10031` **The Train That Outlived the Toy Store**: A toy-shop drawing becomes a miniature railway at the edge of town that the player can ride, outliving the shop that paid for it. It's visible, charming and fits the 2.5D street. The A line is warm; the Gazette bodies ('Real fares and operating costs entered the project accounts') need replacing.
- `scenario-20261117-10784` **The Parent Who Survived and Left**: Warning a parent away from a bridge collapse saves a life but not the marriage. It's the prompt's own 'warn a father' idea with a good bittersweet twist. But the collapse itself and its other victims are left 'to their own recorded history' (a missed bigger rescue), and C's line 'My parent is alive. Our family also changed.' reads like a form.
- `scenario-20261117-9571` **The Face Missing from the Class Picture**: A person who never existed is born and paints the town mural: a top-tier ripple, with a careful four-step chain and birth rule. But the hook contradicts its own title ('A school photograph has no missing or ghostly face'), the copy reads 'The birth entered the actual household and school-age population history', and the twist (a merchant moves an advertisement) is weak.
- `scenario-20261117-9713` **The Debt Behind the Anniversary Notice**: Real gossip with moral weight, built almost entirely from existing levers (rumor, knows, bond, divorce, marry), and B's line 'I still wish I had heard the truth privately' is good. Tighten the template timing: the divorce comes 12-14 years after the reveal and the remarriage at 58-65.
- `scenario-20261117-10781` **The Bank That Faced the Parking Lot**: A great fit for a walkable 2.5D street: the bank turns its door to a drive-up lot, and the clothing store next door dies of lost foot traffic. B's line ('The bank did not move away. It only turned around.') is excellent. The Gazette bodies are boilerplate, and relocate_entrance needs renderer support.
- `scenario-20261117-10172` **The Job Left Empty**: A rare cruel act that helps someone: the man who got the murdered mechanic's job later employs the orphan, and D's line ('It is a good job. That is a different thing from the parent I do not have.') is strong. It uses standard levers (kill, hire, found_business). It needs a reason for the player to want this, and the moralizing bodies ('Hiring did not replace the person the family lost') should go.

Prompt v2 (`docs/CHATGPT_TIMETRAVEL_PROMPT.md`) answers each point with hard rules, a closed effect list, the game's real
business kinds and town facts, banned words, and a worked example in the right voice.

## Content batches (64 items)

Verdicts: 40 keep, 23 keep with edits, 1 reject (rumor-20261117-178: no florist in the game, same premise as rumor-6). Good, warm, specific
writing. To fix when importing: six of seven shop items name kinds the game lacks (hardware store, barbershop, diner,
repair shop, general store, florist); several items assume a church, mill, river or bridge many towns don't have;
business losses hit the owner's money instead of the shop's; five bodies hard-code "the Gazette" (the engine renames
the paper after 1990); no contractions in dialogue; 55 of 64 items are the same in every decade. The ids skip (1-8,
then 149-228 with gaps), so more items were written than committed. The content prompt now carries these fixes.

## What using them needs (engine side)

The simulation today has the six verbs applied once in their year (src/sim/simulate.ts applyIntervention) and a full
re-simulation from 1950 (runTimeline, about 1 s on the dev server, not measured on the S25). Scenarios need: a loader
and validator, role binding with town-relative wealth/reputation bands, offers placed in every timeline (so the clues
exist before you travel), a ripple scheduler keyed by (seed, scenario, year, step), and about a dozen engine
primitives (business found/close, marriage/child, office, presence (people leaving town), sites for build/tear_down,
pay, save from a death, secrets/threads). The Android app doesn't run the simulation yet (it reads exported
street.json and dialogue.json), so time travel on the phone also needs the simulation on the device.
