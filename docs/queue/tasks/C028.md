# C028: Owning a car (buying, papers, fuel and breakdowns)

- Type: engineering

- Status: open

## Why it matters
The game has drivable cars and no fast travel, so owning a car is a big step, and it should cost what it did. A car
needs buying (new or used), papers (registration and insurance by era), fuel, and repairs. It breaks down, sometimes on
an empty road at night, which the owner wants as part of the journey.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 a used 1946 sedan is $450 at the lot on the highway. The registration is
another $12. Two weeks later the radiator boils over on the road out of town, forty miles from the nearest garage.

## What to build: `src/cars/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **The market:** `carsForSale(year, seed)` lists new and used cars by era from a small authored list of body types
   (sedan, coupe, pickup, wagon, sports car), with ages, mileage, condition and asking prices. Use the economy's
   `priceIndex` and price curves (import them from `src/economy/`, don't edit it). Older and worse cars are cheaper.
   The game maps body types to its own car models.
2. **Papers by era:** registration, a driving licence, and insurance (required from a given era), with yearly costs and
   what happens without them: a police stop can fine you or take the car.
3. **Running costs:** fuel use by body type and era (miles per gallon) priced with the economy's gasoline price, plus
   oil, tyres and servicing.
4. **Breakdowns:** `drive(car, miles, conditions)` updates mileage and condition and may return a breakdown (a flat,
   overheating, a dead battery, engine failure), more likely when the car is old, worn or unserviced, or the weather is
   hot or cold. `repairCost(breakdown, year)`, and whether you can fix it at the roadside or need a tow.
5. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/CARS.md` with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs, no
   `Date.now` or `Math.random` (use `rand` from `src/sim/rng.ts`).

## Tests
- Used cars cost less than new ones.
- Insurance appears from its era.
- A neglected old car breaks down more often over 1,000 miles than a serviced new one.
- Fuel cost per mile changes with the era's gas price.
- Determinism.
