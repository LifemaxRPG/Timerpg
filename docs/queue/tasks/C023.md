# C023: Jobs: openings, hiring and getting fired

- Assignee: chatgpt
- Type: engineering
- Branch: `codex/C023-jobs`
- Status: open

## Why it matters
The playable loop pays for one-off shifts. Legit money in a grounded game means a real job: someone has to hire you,
you show up on time, and you can lose it. A job is also how the town comes to know the player (a coworker, a boss),
and what a theft can cost them.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 the hardware store has a card in the window: "Stock boy wanted, inquire
within." The owner asks where the player last worked. With no reference, it's a week's trial at 60¢ an hour. Late twice,
and they're let go.

## What to build: `src/jobs/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **Openings:** `openings(places, year, day, seed)` lists jobs on offer by place kind and era (shop clerk, stock boy,
   dishwasher, mechanic's helper, labourer, night clerk), with pay from the economy's `wage(job, year)` (import it from
   `src/economy/`), hours, and what the employer wants (age, references, papers by era).
2. **Hiring:** `apply(opening, applicant)` takes the applicant's age, references, known record (what the town knows of
   them, as a reputation from 0 to 1 that the game supplies) and papers. It returns hired, a trial, or refused, with a
   plain reason.
3. **Keeping it:** `shift(job, record)` updates a job record from each shift (on time, late, absent, caught stealing).
   It returns warnings, a raise after steady weeks, or dismissal, and a reference the player can carry (good, poor or
   none).
4. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/JOBS.md` with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs, no
   `Date.now` or `Math.random` (use `rand` from `src/sim/rng.ts`).

## Tests
- Openings vary by era and place.
- A good reference beats no reference.
- Lateness leads to a warning, then dismissal, on a known schedule.
- A raise after steady weeks.
- Determinism.
