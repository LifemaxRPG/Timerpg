## Why it matters
For much of the century, a town's choice was its shops plus the catalogue: you ordered by mail and it came weeks later
to the post office or the station. Later came phone orders and, much later, buying online. It's another way to get goods,
it leaves a paper trail (a name and an address on an order), and it's something a time traveller can use or be caught
by.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1925, the player can't find a revolver for sale in town. The catalogue at the
general store lists one for $14.50, mailed in three weeks to General Delivery. The order slip carries the name they gave.

## What to build: `src/mailorder/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
- **Catalogues by era:** what's in them (a wider range than town shops: tools, clothes, household goods, a whole house
  kit in its era), prices through the economy (import `priceIn`/`priceIndex`; don't edit `src/economy/`), and what can't
  be mailed by era (firearms rules, liquor in Prohibition).
- **Ordering and delivery:** `placeOrder(items, buyer, address, year, day, method)` by mail, then phone, then online by
  era. Delivery time uses your comms and transit timing (import them; don't edit them). Pickup at the post office or
  station, or home delivery by era.
- **The trail:** each order keeps a record (the name given, the address, payment by money order, cash on delivery,
  cheque or card, by era) as plain facts the game can pass to the crime code as evidence.
- Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/MAILORDER.md`. No Node or browser APIs, no `Date.now` or
  `Math.random` (use `rand`).

## Tests
- Delivery takes weeks in 1925 and days later.
- Restricted items are refused by era.
- The order record carries the given name and payment.
- Online ordering only from its era.
- Determinism.
