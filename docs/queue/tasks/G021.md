## Why it matters
Consequences travel through people (CLAUDE.md). The loop already knows who saw what, who heard it from whom, who the
player worked with and who stopped them. The NPC dialogue model only knows what its fact sheet says. This task turns
the loop's events into first-person memories on each NPC's fact sheet, so a clerk who saw the theft brings it up, a
coworker greets the player by name, and a gossip passes on what they heard, hedged.

## What to build
1. **`memoriesFor(personId, events, now)` in `src/llm/`:** from a plain list of loop events (the shape `src/loop`
   returns: witnessed crimes, heard rumours with who told them, shifts worked together, stops, arrests, trials,
   purchases, kindness), the 3–6 most relevant first-person lines for that person, e.g. "I saw you take a watch from
   Fairchild's on Tuesday" or "Lois told me you were picked up by the police, but I don't know if it's true".
2. **How sure they are:** saw it > heard it from a friend > heard it around; time fades it; their traits shade it
   (a loyal friend plays it down, a gossip plays it up). Never invent: every line traces to an event.
3. **Into the brief:** `brief()`/`withRecent()` take the memories so the model sees them, and `validate()` keeps
   rejecting a reply that states something as seen when the NPC only heard it.
4. **Plain data only:** the function takes and returns plain JSON so the phone can call it through the bridge.

## Tests
- A witness's sheet has the seen line; a friend who was told has the hedged one; a stranger has nothing.
- Memories fade with time and rank by relevance; at most 6.
- Every line maps back to its event id.
- The existing factsheet and conversation-log tests stay green.
