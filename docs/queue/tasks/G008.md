## Why it matters
Your G005 crime core spreads what people *saw*. Real cases also rest on what was *left behind*, and the owner wants
forensics that follow the era: fingerprints, then databases, DNA and cameras, plus cold cases solved decades later.
Time travel gives this a twist: a player's prints can turn up "before they were born". See docs/IDEAS.md (crime,
forensics) and docs/CRIME.md (your design).

## What to build: `src/crime/evidence.ts` (and new files beside it), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **Traces.** A crime event can leave traces:
   - fingerprints (on items and surfaces touched, unless gloved);
   - footprints;
   - blood or DNA (rarely);
   - a dropped item;
   - stolen goods with serial numbers (by item kind and era);
   - a vehicle seen or a plate;
   - security-camera footage, where a camera covered the spot (the caller supplies camera positions).
   Each trace has a quality that decays with time and weather, and can be destroyed (cleaned, burned) by anyone.
2. **Science by era.** What the police can do with each trace, by year:
   - fingerprints matched by hand from about 1910, against a local card file;
   - a national database later;
   - automated matching from the 1980s;
   - DNA from the late 1980s, with databases later;
   - camera footage from the 1970s, in colour and widespread later;
   - plates only once vehicles are registered.
   Keep the years as data in one table so they're easy to tune.
3. **Linking.** Traces link to a person only if that person is "on file" in that jurisdiction and era: arrested before,
   a licence, a database entry. Otherwise a trace sits in the case file unmatched, and can match later when the person
   enters the files. That's the cold case: solved years later when the science or the files catch up. Use your
   existing jurisdictions and case files.
4. **Time travel.** A player-traveller's prints can be on file in a year *before* their birth date, or before they
   ever arrived in that era. Expose a flag so the game can react (a baffled detective, a newspaper oddity). Leave the
   reaction to the game; just detect it.
5. **Combine with your witness merge.** A case's strength combines witness descriptions and physical evidence into
   one score that the future trial system will use. Expose `caseStrength(case, day)` with its parts, so a prosecutor
   could explain them.

- The "prints before birth" flag.
- Cleaning destroys traces.
- Determinism and JSON round-trip.
- The old G005 tests still pass.
- Performance stays inside the 5 ms `advanceDay` budget for 2,000 people and 20 cases.
