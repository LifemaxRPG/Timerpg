## Why it matters
NPC replies now come fast on the phone, but they aren't always good. From a real play session's log:
- an NPC got stuck on one subject (the same rumour about the same person three replies in a row);
- an NPC went straight to "shut up before I call the police" with no reason in the facts;
- 5 of 21 turns fell back to pre-written lines.

## What to build (in `src/llm/`)
1. **Follow the conversation:** the brief carries the last few turns as a short summary (who said what, what was
   already told) so the model answers the player's actual question and doesn't repeat a topic it covered.
2. **Topic variety:** a rumour or fact that was just told is marked as told; the next reply on the same topic adds
   something new or says they've nothing more.
3. **Mood that fits:** the NPC's mood toward the player comes from the facts (attitude, memories from G021, the
   time of day, what the player said) and is stated plainly in the brief, with how strongly to show it; no hostility
   without a reason in the facts.
4. **Fewer fallbacks:** look at why replies fail `validate()` most often (run the existing evaluation, see
   kotlin-street/docs/CONVERSATION.md) and fix the brief or the rule, not by loosening checks that protect quality.
5. **An evaluation:** a set of multi-turn conversations (at least 20) with the expected behaviour, scored
   automatically where possible, before and after.
