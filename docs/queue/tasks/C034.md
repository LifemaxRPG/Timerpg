## Why it matters
Witnesses remember the player's height, build, hair and the colour of their clothes, and the police merge those into a
description (Jeff's crime code, `src/crime/types.ts`: `CulpritAppearance`, `ClothingColor`, `MEMORY_DROP_ORDER`). The
easiest thing to change is clothes, which is also the first detail witnesses forget. A grounded crime game needs a
wardrobe: buy a different coat, a hat, glasses, dye your hair, and the description stops fitting, at a price and with
its own risk (a shop clerk remembers who bought a red coat the day after the robbery).

**The player's first encounter:** the paper says police seek "a man in a grey coat". The player buys a brown coat and a
hat at the clothing store for $14 (1952) and walks past an officer who only glances. The clerk, though, remembers the
nervous customer who paid cash.

## What to build: `src/clothes/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **A wardrobe by era:** coats, jackets, hats, glasses, scarves, work clothes and uniforms (by era and fashion), each
   with a colour from `ClothingColor`, a price (the economy's `priceIn`/`priceIndex`; import it, don't edit
   `src/economy/`), and which shop kinds sell it.
2. **Appearance from what's worn:** `appearanceOf(base, outfit)` turns the player's fixed traits (height, build, hair)
   plus the outfit into a `CulpritAppearance` (import the type from `src/crime/`; don't edit it). Hats and hair dye can
   change or hide hair; glasses and scarves lower how recognisable the face is, as a plain number the game passes to the
   witness code.
3. **How well a description fits:** `matchScore(description, appearance)` from 0 to 1, so the game can see that
   changing coats lowers the match. Keep it simple and documented; the police code has its own recognition.
4. **Purchases leave a trail:** `purchaseRecord(item, day, shop)` says what a clerk might remember (cash, nervousness,
   buying right after a crime), as plain facts the game passes to the witness and gossip code.
5. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/CLOTHES.md` with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs,
   no `Date.now` or `Math.random` (use `rand` from `src/sim/rng.ts`).

## Tests
- A coat change lowers `matchScore` against a description that names the old colour.
- A hat hides hair colour.
- Items appear by era.
- Prices scale by era.
- Determinism.
