## Why it matters
The owner's idea (docs/IDEAS.md, "Trials with a jury"): if the player is charged, the case goes to court, with a judge,
a prosecutor, a defence and a jury of real townspeople. The evidence is what really happened. Your G005 and G008 already
produce the case and its strength (witnesses plus physical evidence). Your G009, police in action, makes the arrests.
This task decides what happens in court. The language model will voice it later; your code decides it.

## What to build: `src/court/`, pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **Charges** from the case: the crime kinds, their severity, the era's law (a small table of crimes, sentence
   ranges and bail by era).
2. **Pre-trial:**
   - bail (by severity, flight risk, wealth and record);
   - a plea offer by case strength (a weak case gives a better deal);
   - the defence lawyer, by money: a public defender with a low budget and less preparation, up to an expensive lawyer
     with high preparation.
3. **Jury selection.** Draw 12 jurors from the jurisdiction's adults. Each juror has the simulation's traits and
   affinity with the defendant and the victim, and their own knowledge (gossip about the case, from G005). Both sides
   may strike a few. Expose the voir-dire facts so the game can show them.
4. **The trial as a sequence of events:**
   - prosecution witnesses and evidence (from `caseStrength`'s parts);
   - cross-examination, where a mistaken witness detail can be exposed (G005 descriptions carry their errors) and
     contradicting evidence weakens the case (your `witness_physical_conflict`);
   - the defence case;
   - the player's own testimony as an input score, which the game will compute from the spoken defence.
5. **Deliberation and verdict.** Each juror's belief in guilt comes from the case strength they perceived, their
   traits, their biases and the defence's quality. Then guilty, not guilty, or a hung jury that triggers a retrial.
   Sentence within the era's range by severity, record and the judge's traits.
6. **Appeals and exoneration:** a later DNA match or a new witness can overturn a conviction (your exoneration idea).
7. **Crimes around the trial,** as inputs with consequences:
   - bribing or threatening a juror or witness;
   - perjury.
   These go through your crime core as new crimes.

## Tests (`npx vitest run src/court`)
Deterministic scenarios:
- a strong-evidence robbery leads to a conviction and a sentence in range;
- a single mistaken eyewitness against DNA leads to an acquittal;
- an expensive lawyer measurably raises acquittal odds over many seeds (print the rates);
- jurors who know the victim are struck;
- a hung jury leads to a retrial;
- a 1925 versus 1995 trial differs (the evidence available);
- a bribed juror changes the outcome but creates a new crime case;
- exoneration years later.
