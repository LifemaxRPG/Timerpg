# G009: Police in action (patrols, searches, spotting, arrests), built on your plan language

- Assignee: gemini
- Type: engineering
- Branch: `gemini/G009-police-action`
- Status: open

## Why it matters
Your crime core tells the police what they know, and your plan language lets NPCs act. This task joins them: officers
who patrol, look for suspects, recognise them, question witnesses, give chase and make arrests. It all runs as plans
through `PlanWorld`, so the game can run it in the town. This is the missing half of "the police look for the player".

## What to build: `src/police/`, pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **The force by jurisdiction and era:**
   - headcount from town size;
   - beats or patrol routes over the town's streets (given as a graph by the caller);
   - shifts, foot or car by era, radio from about 1930;
   - a detective for serious cases.
2. **Behaviour as plans.** Each officer's day is generated as plans in the G007 language:
   - patrol a beat;
   - answer a reported crime (go there, question the witnesses: your G005 interview refreshes their memory);
   - search for a wanted suspect where they were last seen or are likely to be (home, haunts);
   - stake out.
3. **Spotting.** Each tick, officers who see a person test `recognitionChance` (G005) against wanted descriptions.
   - Above a threshold they act by the era and the crime's severity: approach and question, ask for papers (by era), or
     call for backup.
   - Lookalikes can be stopped by mistake (your own idea).
4. **Chase and arrest.** A fleeing suspect triggers pursuit plans, on foot or in cars (the actual movement is
   `PlanWorld`'s job). Cornered or caught means arrest: hands up, search (inventory: the caller supplies it), cuffs,
   transport to the station. Outcomes are data for the coming jail and trial systems.
5. **Heat drives effort.** High heat in a jurisdiction means more patrols near the crime area and more checks.
   Fleeing to another jurisdiction resets it, unless the case escalates (your G005 escalation).

## Tests (`npx vitest run src/police`)
Scenarios on the stub town, all deterministic:
- a reported robbery brings an officer to the scene, who interviews the witnesses;
- a wanted suspect walking the beat is spotted and questioned;
- the same suspect in different clothes and at night is missed more often (print the rates);
- a fleeing suspect is chased and arrested, or escapes into another jurisdiction;
- a lookalike is stopped and released;
- 1925 versus 1990 behaviour differs (no radio and fewer officers in 1925);
- performance: 30 officers in a 2,000-person town step in under 1 ms per tick.
