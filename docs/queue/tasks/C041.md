## Why it matters
The owner wants government in the game, up to running for office yourself one day (docs/IDEAS.md, "government and
elections"). It starts in the town: a mayor, a council, a police chief who answers to them, and elections on the
calendar. Politics also sets things the player feels: how many officers the town pays for, Sunday closing laws, taxes,
a crackdown after a crime wave.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 the capital's mayoral election is in Leafmonth. The challenger promises "more
police on Main Street" after a run of burglaries. The paper covers the race, and the winner's first act doubles the
night patrol.

## What to build: `src/politics/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
- **Offices by town size and era:** mayor, council seats, police chief or sheriff (appointed or elected by era), with
  terms and election dates on your calendar (import `src/calendar/`; don't edit it).
- **Candidates and platforms:** from a list of the town's people the game supplies (traits, jobs, standing), pick
  candidates and give each a platform on a few issues: policing, taxes, business hours, roads.
- **Elections:** turnout and results from the town's mood. The game supplies plain numbers such as crime this year,
  prices and unemployment, and you return the vote by neighbourhood.
- **Policy effects:** the winner's platform becomes plain settings the game reads: police headcount multiplier, shop-hour
  rules, a tax rate, a crackdown flag.
- Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/POLITICS.md`. No Node or browser APIs, no `Date.now` or
  `Math.random` (use `rand`).

## Tests
- Elections fall on their calendar dates.
- A crime wave raises the law-and-order candidate's vote.
- Policy settings follow the winner.
- Offices change by era.
- Determinism.
