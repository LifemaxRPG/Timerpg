# G011: The loop facade (`src/loop/`): the one API the phone calls

- Assignee: gemini
- Type: engineering
- Branch: `gemini/G011-loop-facade`
- Status: open (start after G009 is delivered)

## Why it matters
The owner approved the team's next milestone: the first playable loop in a few capital blocks
(kotlin-street/docs/LOOP.md). Arrive, earn money legit or not, get noticed, and later meet a consequence you can trace
back to what you did. The phone will run your TypeScript as-is: it's bundled by esbuild into one file and run in an
embedded JavaScript engine (QuickJS) on the phone. So your crime, evidence, plan and police code is exactly what the
player meets. This task is the doorway: one small API, JSON in and JSON out, that the game calls.

**The player's first encounter:** they pocket a tin from the general store while the owner's back is turned, but a
woman at the window sees it. Two days later the owner won't serve them, a neighbour mentions it in conversation, and an
officer is walking that block.

## What to build: `src/loop/`, pure and deterministic, with tests
One module, `src/loop/index.ts`, exporting a `LoopSim` with these calls. Every argument and result is plain JSON
(no class instances, Maps or functions across the boundary), because the phone passes strings.
1. `create(init) -> LoopSim`, where `init` holds:
   - the seed, the era year and the start day and hour;
   - the people: id, age, traits, home, workplace, relationships (kin, spouse, friends with affinity);
   - the places: id, kind (store, home, pawn shop, police station...), position, owner, the jurisdiction;
   - the street graph (nodes and edges with positions) and the street lamps.
2. `act(sim, event) -> ActResult`: the player did something. Kinds, at least:
   - `buy`, `sell` (at the pawn shop: a stolen item is noted, and later can be traced, from your G008 work);
   - `work_shift` (legit pay; the employer and coworkers know the player by name after it);
   - `take` (shoplift, from a yard, from a house, a wallet), `take_vehicle`;
   - `enter` (a place, open or not: trespass and break-in when it isn't).
   The event carries the scene snapshot at that moment: the bystanders (id, position, facing, what they were
   doing), the light, and the player's appearance (clothes, build, vehicle). Use your `witnessesOf` to decide who saw
   it, and `recordCrime` for crimes. Return who saw it and how well (the game shows nothing of this to the player,
   but it's useful for the debug overlay).
3. `advance(sim, hours) -> Changes`: run gossip, reporting with the era's delay, and your G009 police plans for that
   much time. Return what changed, in a form the game can act on:
   - new police plans to act out (an officer's route, a stake-out, "question person X");
   - people whose attitude to the player changed.
4. Queries the game calls when it needs them:
   - `attitude(sim, personId)`: what this person knows of the player and how they feel: `serve`, `wary`, `refuse`,
     `report_on_sight`, with the reasons as short facts in plain English. Those facts go into the dialogue model's
     fact sheet, for example "You saw this person steal a tin of peaches from Hale's store on Tuesday".
   - `policeView(sim, jurisdictionId)`: the merged description, heat and case strength for each open case.
   - `spotted(sim, officerId, sighting) -> SpotResult`: an officer sees the player. Use your `recognitionChance`,
     and return what they do (ignore, watch, approach and question, arrest).
5. `arrest(sim, ...)` for an outcome: for the loop, a fine or a night in a cell by severity and record. Your G010
   court comes after this and replaces it.
6. `save(sim) -> string` and `load(string) -> LoopSim`: the whole state as JSON, round-tripping exactly.

## Rules for code that runs on the phone
- No Node or browser APIs (no `fs`, `process`, `Buffer`, timers, `console` beyond `console.log`), and no `Date.now`
  or `Math.random`: use `rand(seed, ...keys)` as you already do.
- ES2020 is fine. Keep imports to `src/` (no npm packages).
- `advance(sim, 24)` for 300 people should take well under 50 ms in Node; the phone's JS engine is roughly 20-50x
  slower than Node's, and the game runs it in a background thread in bursts. Report your Node timings.

## Tests
A stub capital of a few blocks: a general store and its owner, a market, a pawn shop, a police station, a street of
homes, 40-60 people with families and friendships. At minimum:
- the first-encounter scenario above, end to end, through the public API only;
- an unseen theft leaves no one aware;
- a legit shift leaves the employer saying the player's name, and no police interest;
- selling a stolen item at the pawn shop can lead back to the player;
- `save`/`load` round-trips and continues identically;
- the same seed and inputs give identical results.
