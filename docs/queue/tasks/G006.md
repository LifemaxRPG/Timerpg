# G006: Signs that say the owner's name (the sign painter)

- Assignee: gemini
- Type: engineering
- Branch: `gemini/G006-sign-painter`
- Status: accepted

## Why it matters
The same hand-made building appears in many towns and many eras, so its sign can't have one fixed name. The owner
wants every sign to show the real business: the NPC owner's name ("Hollis Barber", "Pruitt & Sons"), changing when
the business changes hands or the era changes. Today names are baked into the textures, or chosen from a few
pre-made variants (the funeral home's six). This task builds the runtime painter that writes any name onto any sign.

## Which buildings (the owner's decision)
Only **standard** buildings get owner names: the kinds that repeat across towns, such as shops, strip malls, the
barber, the diner, gas stations, the funeral home, motels and stores. **Unique** buildings keep their own fixed names
and appear in only one town: landmarks and monuments, the courthouse, the ballpark, the mini golf, the Great Meridian.
A model's `model-info.json` says which with `"naming": "owner"` or `"naming": "fixed"` (default `fixed`).
`SignSpec` reads it, and a `fixed` model is never repainted.

## What to build (new files only, in plain Kotlin with no Android or Filament code, so it runs and tests on the JVM)
`kotlin-street/shared/src/main/kotlin/domain/signs/`:
1. **`PixelFont`:**
   - pixel-art bitmap fonts drawn on the art-pixel grid, in at least three era styles:
     - a 1900-1940s serif-block face;
     - a 1950s-60s rounded face (diner and motel style);
     - a 1970s-today plain sans.
   - upper case, lower case for the sans only, digits, `& . , ' - !`;
   - each glyph defined in code as rows of pixels;
   - look at `kotlin-street/tools/landmarks/pixel_font.py` (the faces we already use offline) and match its proportions.
2. **`SignSpec`:** parsed from a model's `model-info.json` `signs` list, each with:
   - `id`, `texture` (which image), `rect` [x, y, w, h] in texels;
   - `lines` (1-2), `maxChars`;
   - `face` (era style, or "auto");
   - `ink`, `paper` and optional `outline` colours;
   - `align`;
   - `glow` (whether the sign's glow mask should follow the letters).
   Also accept the older `sign-regions.json` shape (name → [x, y, w, h]), with defaults.
3. **`SignPainter.paint(spec, text, era, out: IntArray, stride)`:**
   - writes the lettering into an RGBA pixel buffer region, centred, scaled by whole pixels only (1x, 2x, 3x), never
     blurred;
   - shrinks or wraps to two lines to fit, and abbreviates as a last resort ("& Sons" before "and Sons", then initials);
   - paints the paper first, so the old baked name is fully covered;
   - also writes the matching glow-mask pixels when `glow`.
   - Zero allocation per paint after warm-up.
4. **`SignNames`:** names from the simulation's facts. `nameFor(kind, ownerSurname, ownerFirst, era, seed)` gives a
   business name in the style of its era and kind:
   - "Hollis Barber Shop", "Pruitt & Sons Funeral Home", "Bob's Diner", "Marlow's Five and Dime", "Ashby Hair Co.",
     "Kessler Nail Room";
   - deterministic from the seed, with a few patterns per kind and era;
   - no real brand names.

## Tests (plain JVM; you can run these: `gradle :desktop:crossroadsTest --tests 'SignPainterTest'`)
- Every glyph is the stated height in pixels, and no glyph pixel falls outside the rect.
- A long name shrinks or wraps instead of overflowing; the old name is fully covered.
- Names are deterministic per seed.
- No allocation per paint after warm-up.
- A proof sheet PNG written by a test: about 20 signs painted onto blank boards at real sizes, all three faces, short
  and long names, one- and two-line. Look at it and fix what reads badly.

If the gradle task won't run in your environment, deliver with "tests not run here: <why>" and Claude runs them.
