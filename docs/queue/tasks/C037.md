## Why it matters
Money in a grounded world also moves at the card table and the bookie's: a poker game in the back of the pool hall, a
bet on Saturday's ball game (the radio carries it: your C031), the numbers racket, slot machines where they're legal.
It's a fast way to gain or lose, with its own legal line by era, and people who remember debts.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 a regular at the diner says there's a game in the back of the hall on Friday
nights. The buy-in is $5. The player wins $22, and the man who lost it won't forget their face.

## What to build: `src/gambling/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **Games:** a few simple games resolved by odds (a five-card draw hand as a small authored simulation, dice, a
   wheel, a slot machine), each fair or crooked (a rigged game shifts the odds; a skilled player can spot it). Use
   `rand(seed, ...)` with keys the game supplies.
2. **Betting:** odds on the town's ball games and horse races (generated fixtures with results the radio and paper can
   report). The bookie's cut. The numbers racket: a daily three-digit number, its payout, and the runner who collects.
3. **The law by era:** what's legal where (a table: card rooms, lotteries, slots, betting), so the game knows when a
   game is a crime to run or join; plus debts: who owes whom, and how a collector escalates (a warning, a visit, worse;
   as plain events the game passes on).
4. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/GAMBLING.md` with an example of each call. Use the economy's
   `priceIndex` for stakes (import it; don't edit `src/economy/`). No Node or browser APIs, no `Date.now` or
   `Math.random`.

## Tests
- A fair game's long-run return is close to its odds over many seeds.
- A rigged game returns less.
- Legality changes by era.
- A debt escalates on schedule.
- Determinism.
