# C040: Pockets, bags and hiding places (what the player carries)

- Assignee: chatgpt
- Type: engineering
- Branch: `codex/C040-carrying`
- Status: open

## Why it matters
The playable loop keeps a flat list of items. A grounded crime game cares where things are: a watch in a pocket, a radio
under an arm (everyone sees it), a tyre iron in the car's trunk, cash in a sock. The owner decided there's no weight
slowdown. But what you carry, and how well it's hidden, matters when a shopkeeper, a witness or a police search looks
at you.

**The player's first encounter:** leaving the hardware store with a stolen hand drill under the coat, the bulge shows.
An officer's stop finds it in a pat-down. Had it been in the car's trunk, it would have needed a search warrant (by era).

## What to build: `src/carrying/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
- **Containers:** pockets (by outfit: use your clothes module, imported, not edited), a bag or briefcase, a coat's
  inside pocket, a car's glovebox and trunk, a rented room's drawer, a home's hiding place. Each with a size and how
  concealed it is.
- **Items:** take each item's size from the economy catalogue's weight class (`pocket`, `handheld`, `bulky`, `heavy`;
  import it).
  - `place(item, container)` refuses what doesn't fit.
  - `visibility(carried)` says what an onlooker sees: nothing, a bulge, or the item in plain view.
- **Searches by era:** a pat-down finds pocket and coat items, a bag search opens bags, a car search needs cause or a
  warrant by era, and a room search needs a warrant. `searchFinds(carried, searchKind, year, seed)` lists what's found.
- Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/CARRYING.md`. No Node or browser APIs, no `Date.now` or
  `Math.random` (use `rand`).

## Tests
- A bulky item can't go in a pocket.
- A coat hides a handheld item as a bulge.
- A pat-down finds pocket items but not the trunk.
- Era rules for car searches.
