# G017: The timeline engine (the time-travel rules as a playable system)

- Assignee: gemini
- Type: engineering (TypeScript)
- Branch: `gemini/G017-timeline`
- Status: accepted

## Why it matters
Time travel is the game's signature feature, and its rules are decided (docs/IDEAS.md, "The time-travel rules:
travellers are unanchored"). The world is already "the seed plus interventions": `src/sim/` replays a town's history
from a seed, `applyIntervention` lets a time traveller change it, and `src/sim/diff.ts` compares two timelines. What's
missing is the layer the game actually plays with. It needs to know:
- what the player changed;
- what the present looks like after each jump;
- who remembers what;
- what happens to evidence of a crime committed in a past that no longer happened;
- what happens to the player who erases their own past.

This is a big task, and it's yours from start to finish. It is one deliverable, a single module.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 the player burns down the hardware store. They jump back to 2025. The store's
lot is a parking garage now, the owner's grandson who sold the player a coffee this morning was never born, and the
police file for the arson is a 73-year-old cold case. But the player still remembers the grandson, and still has the
receipt in their pocket for a coffee that was never sold.

## What to build: `src/timeline/` (a new module), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **The timeline ledger:** a plain-JSON `Timeline` the game saves.
   - It holds the seed and every intervention the player made, each with its year, place and kind.
   - `branch(timeline, intervention)` returns the new timeline and leaves the old one unchanged.
   - Map the player's crimes and acts (Jeff's crime kinds in `src/crime/types.ts`; import them, don't edit them) to
     sim interventions. Add new intervention kinds in your module, wrapping the sim's.
2. **The present after a jump:** `presentAt(timeline, year)` replays the sim with the interventions. Cache it by
   (seed, intervention list, year) so a jump costs one replay.
   - It returns the world plus a `changes` report: who was erased, who is new, businesses that never opened or never
     closed, owners and mayors, and buildings demolished or never built.
   - Build it on `src/sim/diff.ts` (import it; don't edit `src/sim/`).
3. **Anchoring (rules 1-3):**
   - The player and what they carry are unanchored: items in their inventory keep their history even if their maker
     never existed (the receipt above).
   - Everyone else re-forms: their memories of the player come only from the current timeline.
   - `selfErase(timeline, year)`: the player removes their own anchored self. Their identity facts go (birth record,
     bank account, fingerprints on file, family who know them), and the report says what they lose (references, legal
     identity) and gain (no prints on file).
4. **Evidence across time:** a crime's evidence (Jeff's `src/crime`, `src/police`, `src/court`; import them) lives in
   the year it was made and ages forward. Cases go cold by era, records are lost in fires and floods, and statutes of
   limitations run out.
   - `caseStatusAt(timeline, crimeId, year)` answers whether the police in that year still look for the player for an
     old crime, with plain reasons.
   - Erasing the witness erases the testimony. Erasing the crime's victim before the crime makes the crime never have
     happened.
5. **Paradox handling:** an intervention that removes the cause of the player's own trip is allowed (the player is
   unanchored), but the report flags it as a "loose thread". Example: the professor's lab never built, which the rules
   allow because he built it as an unanchored man.
   - `looseThreads(timeline)` lists them as plain facts for dialogue and the journal.
6. **Unanchored people:** the professor and any other travellers are stored outside the sim, with a list of the
   (year, place, trace) marks they chose to leave. Changes never remove those traces.
   - `tracesAt(timeline, year, placeId)` returns the traces there.
   - Generate the professor's trail from the seed: 5 to 9 traces across eras and continents, each pointing loosely to
     the next. He's never required to be found.
7. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/TIMELINE.md` with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs,
   no `Date.now` or `Math.random` (use `rand` from `src/sim/`). Keep a replay under about 200 ms for a capital-sized
   town on your machine, and say what you measured.

## Tests (Vitest)
- `branch` never mutates; the same timeline replays the same present.
- An arson in 1952 makes the store's history change by 2025, and the report names it.
- The unanchored player's carried item survives the erasure of its maker.
- `selfErase` removes the player's records and the report lists the losses and gains.
- A 1952 arson is a cold case in 2025 and an active one in 1953; erasing the only witness ends the testimony.
- Erasing the victim before the crime means the crime never happened.
- A loose thread is reported, not refused.
- The professor's traces survive every intervention, and the same seed gives the same trail.
- The cache returns the same present without a second replay.
- At least one test per bullet; the existing `src/sim`, `src/crime`, `src/police` and `src/court` tests stay green.
