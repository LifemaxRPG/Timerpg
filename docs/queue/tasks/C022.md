# C022: The cost of living (rent, food, upkeep)

## Why it matters
In the playable loop, money only goes up: nothing costs the player anything to live. A grounded game needs a reason
for money to run out: a room to rent, meals to eat, a car's gas and repairs, a fine to pay. That's what makes a shift,
or a theft, feel necessary.

**The player's first encounter:** it's 1952. The landlady at the boarding house on Elm Street wants $8 for the week,
paid Saturday. Three meals at the diner are another $2 a day. The player has $11.

## What to build: `src/economy/living.ts`, pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **Lodging by era and kind:** a boarding-house room, a hotel room, a rented flat, a motel room (from about the 1950s).
   Rent per night, week or month; deposits; what's included (meals at a boarding house).
2. **The cost of daily needs:** meals (diner, grocery-cooked, boarding-house board), clothes wear, laundry, carfare and
   transit, a telephone call, a car's running costs (gas through your price curves, repairs, insurance and a licence
   from the eras they apply).
3. **A ledger:** `dueOn(lodging, startDay, day)` says what's owed on a given day. `arrears(ledger)` says how far behind
   the player is, and what follows: the landlord warns, then locks them out and keeps their things. Plain JSON the game
   saves.
4. **Daily budgets for NPCs** by income bracket and era (poor, working, comfortable). The simulation can use them later,
   and the game can show "most people here live on about $X a week".
5. Plain JSON in and out, documented in docs/ECONOMY.md with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs, no
   `Date.now` or `Math.random`.

## Tests
- 1952 boarding-house board and lodging sit in a sensible range (state it).
- A motel room isn't available in 1925.
- Arrears go from a warning to a lockout on a known schedule.
- Car costs exist only from the eras they apply.
- Determinism.
- The economy's existing tests stay green.
