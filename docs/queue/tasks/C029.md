## Why it matters
The game's direction says money comes in legit and nefarious ways, "jobs and businesses". The player can now work a
job; next is owning the shop. A small business is the long game of legit money: buy or lease a storefront, stock it,
hire, set the hours, and watch the week's takings against the costs. It also gives NPC owners real books, which crime
can hurt: a robbery is a loss on the ledger.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 the five-and-dime on Main Street is for sale, $2,400 with its stock, or the
lease is $60 a month. The first week takes in $310 and costs $285, and a broken window from a break-in eats the profit.

## What to build: `src/business/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **For sale or lease:** `businessesForSale(places, year, seed)` lists shops on offer, with an asking price (stock,
   fixtures, goodwill), a lease, and their recent weekly takings.
2. **Running it:** a plain-JSON `Business` state: stock (use the economy's `openStock`/`take`/`restock` and prices),
   staff (wages from `wage`), hours (`hoursOf`), and a cash drawer.
   `runDay(business, day, seed, conditions)` simulates a day's customers by shop kind, era, weekday, holiday and
   weather (take plain modifiers as input) and returns sales, restock costs, wages, rent and the day's profit.
3. **Events on the books:** theft and breakage losses that the game reports as plain events, and the bank or the
   landlord acting on missed payments.
4. **Selling up:** `valuation(business, year)`.
5. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/BUSINESS.md` with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs,
   no `Date.now` or `Math.random` (use `rand` from `src/sim/rng.ts`). Import from `src/economy/` and `src/calendar/`; don't
   edit them.

## Tests
- Takings vary by weekday and drop on holidays when closed.
- A business with too many staff loses money.
- A theft shows as a loss.
- The valuation rises with steady profit.
- Determinism.
