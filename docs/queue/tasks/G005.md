# G005: Witnesses, word of mouth and police reports (the crime system's core)

- Assignee: gemini

- Branch: `gemini/G005-witnesses`

## Why this matters

The owner's direction: crime is grounded. The player can do anything a person could do, with real consequences, and
**consequences travel through people**:
- witnesses remember;
- they tell others according to their traits and relationships;
- someone who will report it does;
- the police in that jurisdiction then know and look for the player;
- fleeing to another continent to hide out is a real option.

This task builds that chain as a pure, deterministic simulation module, the heart of the crime system. Rendering, AI
dialogue and arrests come later and read from what you build.

## What to build: a new module `src/crime/` (TypeScript)

Read first:
- `src/sim/types.ts` (Person, Traits, World);
- `src/sim/social.ts` (`affinity`);
- `src/sim/knowledge.ts` (secrets: the same idea of "who knows what");
- `src/sim/rng.ts` (`rand(seed, ...keys)`: everything must be a pure function of the seed and inputs, with no
  `Math.random`).

1. **Crime events.** `CrimeEvent`:
   - kind: theft, shoplifting, burglary, assault, vandalism, car theft, robbery, murder;
   - with a severity;
   - where (world position plus a place id), when (year, day, hour), who did it (the player or an NPC), the victim,
     and what was taken or damaged.
2. **Witnessing.** `witnessesOf(event, bystanders)` decides who saw it and how well:
   - from distance, light (the hour, plus street lamps if given), line of sight (a caller-supplied
     `canSee(a, b)`, so tests stub it), attention and age;
   - the output is a `Sighting` per witness, with a confidence of 0..1 and a description of the culprit:
     - height, build, hair, clothing colour, any vehicle;
     - each detail present or missing, and possibly wrong, with a probability by confidence;
   - the victim is always a witness when conscious.
3. **Memory.** A sighting fades over days (confidence decays; details drop out, least striking first) unless it's
   refreshed: the witness sees the culprit again, or is asked by police. Severe crimes fade slower.
4. **Word of mouth.**
   - Each simulated day, a person who knows about a crime may tell people they're close to: spouse and kin, friends by
     `affinity`, coworkers. The chance depends on their charm (talkative), the severity, and how close they are.
   - Retold accounts are **second-hand**, with lower confidence, and can distort details.
   - Gossip stays mostly inside a town. It crosses to other towns only through people who travel or have kin there.
   - Keep a `who knows what, and how` ledger.
5. **Reporting.** Each knower decides whether and when to report, by:
   - severity;
   - their relationship to the victim and to the culprit (people shield friends and kin);
   - their reputation;
   - the era (no phones before about 1900: they walk to the station; a phone at home or in shops after that);
   - a per-person "won't talk to police" chance.
   A report goes to the police of the jurisdiction where the crime happened.
6. **Police knowledge.** Per jurisdiction: the cases, the merged culprit description (combining reports, weighted by
   confidence and first-hand over second-hand) and a **heat** level that decays over time. Expose:
   - `policeKnowledge(jurisdiction, day)`;
   - `isWanted(culpritId, jurisdiction, day)`;
   - `recognitionChance(npc, culprit, day)`: an NPC who knows the description, or who saw the culprit themselves,
     recognising them later.
   Jurisdictions are given by the caller as a function from a place id to a jurisdiction id (town police, county
   sheriff, state), so a crime in one town isn't known in another unless it is reported up or spreads.
7. **Tick API.** `advanceDay(state, world, day)` runs memory, gossip and reporting for one day, deterministically.
   Keep the state small and serializable (plain JSON) for saves.

## Tests (vitest: `npx vitest run src/crime`; you can run these in your environment)

- **Determinism:** the same seed and inputs give identical state, across runs and after a JSON round-trip.
- **Witnessing:** a crime at 2 a.m. in an unlit alley has fewer and weaker witnesses than the same crime at noon on
  Main Street.
- **Memory:** a shoplifting sighting fades to nothing within weeks; a murder sighting doesn't.
- **Gossip:**
  - in a test town of about 200 people, a robbery is known by a plausible share after 1, 7 and 30 days (print the
    curve);
  - a culprit's close friend is less likely to report;
  - second-hand accounts have lower confidence than first-hand ones.
- **Jurisdiction:** the police of town A know and town B's don't, unless a witness travels or has kin in B.
- **Era:** reports arrive slower in 1880 than in 1990.
- **Performance:** `advanceDay` for 2,000 people and 20 active cases takes under 5 ms on your machine (print it).
