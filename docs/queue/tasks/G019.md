## Why it matters
Your court (G010, tuned in G013) is merged, but the loop still resolves an arrest with a stand-in: a fine or cell time
straight away (`arrest()` in `src/loop/facade.ts`, "Stand-in arrest resolution"). This task puts the real court behind
an arrest, so a charge has the weight it would have in real life.

**The player's first encounter:** booked for a burglary on thin evidence, they're charged, make bail, hire the
cheapest lawyer they can afford, and walk into a trial date two weeks later. The jury is out for two rounds.

## What to build
1. **From arrest to court:** when the case is strong enough to charge (your stop policy table decides release
   before this), `arrest()` returns a charge instead of a sentence: the charges, a bail amount in era money (or held
   without bail for the worst), and a first court date in game time.
2. **The player's choices, as plain-JSON calls:** pay bail (or stay in a cell), choose counsel by what they can pay
   (your tiers), plead guilty for a lighter sentence or go to trial. Court-crime acts (bribing or threatening a juror
   or witness, perjury) go through your G010 interference and open G013's crime kinds.
3. **The trial runs on the date:** `advance()` reaching the court date runs the trial with your engine, returns the
   verdict and sentence as plain-English facts, and serves the sentence (jail time through `advance`, fines in cents).
   Missing the date issues a warrant that the police act on.
4. **Keep the stop and the thin-case release as they are.** Keep every public signature; add new calls rather than
   changing old ones, and list them in `docs/LOOP_API.md`.

## Tests
- A thin case is released; a solid one is charged with bail; the worst is held without bail.
- A guilty plea gives a lighter sentence than a guilty verdict on the same case.
- The trial runs on its date and serves its sentence; a missed date gives a warrant.
- Bribing a juror opens a bribery case.
- A save taken between arrest and trial round-trips and continues identically.
