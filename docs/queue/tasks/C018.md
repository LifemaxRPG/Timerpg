# C018: The economy by era (prices, wages, what's for sale)

- Assignee: chatgpt
- Type: engineering
- Branch: `codex/C018-economy`
- Status: accepted

## Why it matters
The first playable loop is in the game: the player buys, steals, sells at the pawn shop and works shifts in the capital
(kotlin-street/docs/LOOP.md). Its economy is a placeholder (`kotlin-street/shared/src/main/kotlin/loop/LoopItems.kt`):
a smooth 3.5%-a-year price curve, a few items per shop, and one wage for every job. The game moves through the 20th
century, so money has to feel right in each era: a nickel coffee in 1925, deflation in the Depression, 1970s
inflation, and no television on a shelf before television exists. This is an engineering task in TypeScript, on the
same terms as Jeff's systems work.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 the general store's shelf has canned peaches at 23¢ and a table
radio at $29.95. The pawnbroker offers $6 for the radio, and $3 once word is out that it's stolen. A shift sweeping the
store pays 75¢ an hour.

## What to build: `src/economy/`, pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **A price index by year, 1880 to 2030**, shaped like real history (wartime inflation, 1930s deflation, the
   1970s-80s inflation, later steadiness) but authored by us: a table with interpolation, not a formula, and no copied
   official series.
2. **An item catalogue:** a few hundred items with a base price in one reference year. Each item has:
   - the year it appears and, where relevant, the year it disappears (no radios before about 1922, no TVs before about
     1946, no pocket calculators before about 1971);
   - its category and the shops that stock it;
   - its weight class;
   - whether it carries a serial number (from the era serials began);
   - what share of its price a pawnbroker pays (perishables: nothing).
3. **Stock by shop kind and era:** general store, grocer/market, hardware, pharmacy, five-and-dime, pawn shop, gas
   station, diner, clothing. What each shop sells in a given year, and roughly how many of each, deterministic from
   `rand(seed, ...)` in `src/sim/`.
4. **Wages by job and era:** odd jobs, shop clerk, labourer, mechanic, clerk, nurse, teacher, police officer. Hourly
   pay, plus how many hours a shift is.
5. **Pawn and fence prices:** what the pawn shop pays and charges, less for hot goods; plus a fence's price (a back-room
   buyer who pays less and asks no questions).
6. **A small API** that's plain JSON in and out, for the phone (it runs in an embedded JavaScript engine: no Node or
   browser APIs, no `Date.now` or `Math.random`):
   - `priceIn(itemId, year)`, `stockOf(shopKind, year, seed)`, `wage(job, year)`, `pawnOffer(itemId, year, hot)`;
   - `formatMoney(cents, year)`, which shows "23¢" or "$29.95" as the era would.

## Tests (Vitest)
- No item is for sale outside its years.
- The index is monotonic where history was and dips in the 1930s.
- Sample prices and wages sit in sensible ranges for 1925, 1952, 1978 and 2025 (state your ranges).
- Stock is deterministic per seed.
- The pawn offer is below the price, and lower when hot.
- `formatMoney` gives the era's style.
