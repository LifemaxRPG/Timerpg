## Why it matters
The world is 8-10 continents with a lot of sea between them. The owner's design: ships are real rides you spend time
on, not loading screens. Crossing to another continent needs a passport by era; without one, the only way across is
sailing in illegally. Fleeing to another continent after a crime is a real option. Each passport only works in its own
era.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1952 the liner from the capital's port to the eastern continent sails Tuesdays and
Fridays, five days at sea. Third class is $85. The purser checks passports at the gangway. A man at the harbour bar says a
freighter's mate takes cash for a bunk, no papers asked.

## What to build: `src/ships/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
- **Routes and timetables** between ports the game supplies (port ids and the sea distance): liners, ferries, freighters
  and later cruise ships by era. Sailing days, crossing times and classes. Fares through the economy's `priceIndex`
  (import it; don't edit `src/economy/`).
- **Passports by era:** when they're required for each crossing, how to get one (where, cost, waiting time, documents:
  a birth record the player may not have in a past era), and that a passport is only valid in its era. A time traveller's
  modern passport is worthless in 1952.
- **Without papers:** stowing away and working a passage on a freighter, with a chance of discovery by era, what
  happens if caught, and landing illegally (the game passes it on to the crime code as an event).
- **Life aboard:** what a day at sea holds by class (meals, a cabin, the deck, other passengers), as plain data the game
  can play.
- Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/SHIPS.md`. No Node or browser APIs, no `Date.now` or `Math.random`.

## Tests
- Liners only in their era.
- Fares by class, in order.
- A passport is required by era and invalid outside its era.
- Stowaway discovery chances by era.
- Determinism.
- At least one test per bullet.
