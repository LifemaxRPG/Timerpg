## Why it matters
The game opens with a letter from the professor. Across the century, people reach each other by letter, telegram,
telephone (party lines, booths, the operator) and later long distance. For a player who can't fast travel, these are
how news crosses continents. They're also evidence: a telegram office keeps a record, and an operator might listen in.

**The player's first encounter:** in 1925 a telegram to the next continent costs 75¢ for ten words and arrives the same
day. A letter takes nine days by ship. The general store's telephone is on a party line, and Mrs Pruitt down the road
listens in.

## What to build: `src/comms/` (a new module of its own), pure and deterministic, with tests
1. **Mail:** postage by era and weight, delivery time by distance and era (ship, rail, air mail from its era), and
   collection times at the post office. `sendLetter(fromPlace, toPlace, year, day)` returns the cost and the delivery
   day.
2. **Telegrams:** cost per word by era and distance, delivery time, and a record kept at the office (who sent what,
   which the game can treat as evidence).
3. **Telephones:** who has one by era (shop, home, the booth on the corner; plain shares by era and income bracket),
   local and long-distance call costs, the operator era, and party lines (the chance another household listens in).
   `call(from, to, year, hour)` returns cost, whether it connects (operator hours), and an eavesdrop chance.
4. Plain JSON in and out, documented in a new `docs/COMMS.md` with an example of each call. No Node or browser APIs, no
   `Date.now` or `Math.random` (use `rand` from `src/sim/rng.ts`). Use the economy's `priceIndex` (import it; don't edit
   `src/economy/`).

## Tests
- Air mail is faster than surface mail, and only from its era.
- Telegrams cost per word.
- Party lines only in their era.
- Long distance costs more than local.
- Determinism.
