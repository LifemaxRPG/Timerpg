# G002 review 1: ocean prototype (revise)

Claude compiled `ocean.mat` with matc on the build server (it compiled first try) and ran `-Dmode=ocean`. The
engineering is excellent:
- the CPU-to-shader parity test;
- the height inversion for buoyancy;
- grid snapping without swimming;
- zero allocations;
- about 20 ms a frame on llvmpipe.

The stepped bands, Bayer-dithered foam and glint are the right *direction* for our pixel-art look.

But the renders show the picture is wrong. Fix these on the same branch:

1. **The ocean is upside down relative to the boat and the camera (must).** In every render, the bottom half of the frame
   (in front of and below the boat) is a flat sky colour (light blue by day, orange at dusk, navy at night), and the
   water fills the top half up to the top edge, with no sky above the horizon. The boat itself is upright. So the ocean's
   projection is flipped vertically against the boat's (`buildDeviceLookMatrix` versus Filament's camera for the
   object-domain material), or the water is being drawn as a ceiling.
   - Fix it so the water lies below the horizon, the sky is above, and the near water is under and in front of the boat.
   - Verify it with a render where the camera looks slightly down at the boat from 6 m up and 20 m behind: water must
     fill the lower part of the frame up to the horizon line.
2. **Coverage to the horizon.** The 320 m grid ends near the boat, leaving the foreground empty, and it can't reach
   the horizon.
   - Add distance rings: the same Gerstner function on progressively coarser rings out to about 5-10 km, with wave
     amplitude faded with distance.
   - Add a far horizon plane in the flat band colour.
   - Keep one or two draw calls and the grid snapping.
3. **It reads as clouds or smoke, not water.** The colour bands are huge swirly blobs.
   - Bands should follow the wave shapes: long parallel swell lines perpendicular to the main wave direction, darker
     troughs and lighter faces, crests picked out with a thin band and dithered foam.
   - Scale the banding to the wavelengths (most bands a few metres wide near the camera).
   - Fade toward a single flat colour with distance (an atmospheric band) so the horizon is calm.
   - Look at how 16-bit-era games drew the sea: a few colours, horizontal lines and sparkle.
4. **The boat:** keep it, but render it sitting in the water with a thin foam ring where the hull meets the surface,
   using the existing Jacobian/height data if easy.
