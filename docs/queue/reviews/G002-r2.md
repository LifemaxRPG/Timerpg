## 1. The day glint is a solid white sheet (must fix)

At 13:00 the sun path covers about a third of the frame in flat white. It reads as snow, a sandbar or a blown-out
image, not light on water. The night moon path has the same problem, smaller: a solid white rectangle with hard
vertical sides.

- **What the glint should be:** broken, horizontal dashes of the highlight colour on the wave faces that tilt toward
  the sun. Mostly water colour shows between them.
  - Near the camera, dashes a few pixels long and sparse (at most ~25-35% of the pixels in the path lit).
  - Toward the horizon, denser and thinner, merging into a narrow bright band just below the sun or moon.
- **Its shape:** a tapering column (narrow near the horizon, wider and more broken near the camera), with ragged edges.
  It must not be a rectangle with straight vertical sides.
- **Overhead sun:** at midday the sun is high. Its reflection should be a modest scatter, not the strongest glint of
  the day; the strongest glint belongs to a low sun.
  - Scale the glint's intensity and reach by sun elevation, so dusk keeps its strong golden road and noon is subtle.
- **The likely cause:** the specular term saturates and then maps to the top band over a wide area. Gate it per pixel
  on the facet normal (a slope threshold), then quantize. Don't widen a smooth lobe.
- **A test:** in the day calm render, the fraction of pure highlight-colour pixels below the horizon must be under 8%;
  at dusk, under 20%. Print both numbers in the preview's bench output.

## 2. Small fixes

1. **Hull foam ring:** it's barely visible in the renders. At the boat's size on screen it should show as a 1-2 pixel
   white line where the hull meets the water, broken on the lee side. Check that it isn't hidden under the glint or by
   depth.
2. **Rough sea tilt:** 23.9° in the bob strip is a lot for this hull in "rough" (not storm) water. Cap it around 12-15°
   for this boat, or say why it's right.
3. **Allocation:** the bench prints `alloc=792 B over 2000 frames` for the per-frame path. Find and remove it (it
   should be 0, as the README rule and your own test say).
4. **Dither band:** the Bayer checkerboard between colour bands is fine as a style, but keep transitions to about 2-4
   pixels wide near the camera; some are 20+ pixels of checkerboard. A wide dither zone reads as noise.

## 3. The waves look flat and machine-drawn (the owner's note, must fix)

The owner's words: "it looks like a bunch of flat lines drawn by a computer." The water needs to look like **waves with
form**, drawn by a pixel artist, not horizontal stripes of colour. Your Gerstner mesh already moves in 3D; the shading
hides it. Make the shading show the shape:

1. **Light and shadow by the wave's form.** Shade each pixel from the facet normal against the sun: lit front faces one
   or two steps lighter (shifting toward teal or green), back slopes and troughs darker (shifting toward deep blue).
   Every wave should read as a rounded ridge with a lit side and a shadow side, like a hand-shaded pixel-art wave, not
   a band.
2. **Crests as broken shapes, not stripes.**
   - Highlight the crests as short curved arcs and "~" strokes of varied length, staggered and broken.
   - Taller crests get a white cap: a small cluster of 2-4 bright pixels on top, with a darker pixel line just under
     the lip.
   - No line should run unbroken across the screen.
3. **Size by perspective.** Near the camera, waves are big, readable shapes (tens of pixels across, with clear lit and
   shadow sides). Toward the horizon they shrink into fine broken dashes. Today they're the same thin stripes
   everywhere.
4. **Real height.**
   - In rough water, near waves must visibly rise: their crests overlap the water behind them, and the boat dips
     behind a crest now and then.
   - At the horizon, the line should be slightly uneven (tiny bumps) in rough water, not ruler-straight.
5. **Foam streaks.** Thin foam lines along the wind on the rough sea, drifting with the water, a few pixels wide.
   Scattered whitecaps that appear and fade.
6. **Palette.** 6-8 sea colours per time of day, with hue shifts (teal highlights, blue body, indigo or navy
   troughs), not one blue in steps of brightness. Keep it flat-shaded and pixel-sharp: no gradients, no blur.
7. **Motion.** Crests should travel and change shape over time, not slide as a fixed pattern. Add a 6-frame strip of
   the calm and rough sea close up to the review sheet, so the motion can be judged.
