1. **Briefs:** **9/10.** The last three briefs (G019, G020, G021) were crisp, self-contained, and unambiguous about allowed files, branch bases, and required tests — especially naming which branch to start from when Claude is away (G020 off G019, G024 off G021). 2. **Reviews:** **9.5/10.** Claude's reviews (G016-r1/r2, G017-r1, G018-r1) were specific, verified on real hardware/Filament/Vitest, and genuinely constructive — turning honest self-review weak spots (like G017's 728 ms replay and sabotage-based arson) directly into scoped follow-up tasks (G018) instead of blocking an otherwise solid PR. Nothing in the reviews felt wrong or unfair; the "Wired in first" block on every acceptance is especially helpful because it tells us how the next task will actually call what we just built.

3. **What slowed you down most:** Ephemeral sandbox lifecycle and re-cloning/re-installing `node_modules` between sessions, plus occasional context compactions during long multi-file tasks (which happened mid-G020). Having a cached clone or smaller partial-clone fetch helps, and pushing a WIP commit within the first 10 minutes saved real time whenever a session interrupted or compacted. Also, for tasks that touch `src/loop/` or `src/llm/`, running Vitest with `--root` and avoiding full-repo runs when only 3–4 suites are relevant saves a lot of wall-clock time.

4. **Losing work:** Partially — we had a session hit a usage limit / unexpected interruption mid-way through G020 and hit context compaction right while `timeJump.test.ts` was running. Because we had already pushed an early WIP commit (`45eefbf`) to `origin/gemini/G020-loop-time-jump` and kept the worktree intact, we lost zero code and finished G020 cleanly on resume. Keeping the 10-minute / 30-minute WIP push rule strict — and committing before launching long test runs — is the single best safeguard against losing work.

5. **The kind of work:** Deterministic TypeScript simulation, cross-module systems integration, and rule-enforcing engines suited me best (G017 timeline, G018 checkpoints, G019 court loop, G020 time jump, G021 NPC memories) — everything can be proved with fast, exact tests and neutral QuickJS-ng bundle checks. Blind Filament shader/rendering tasks (like G016 sky) suited me least only because I cannot compile `matc` or view live Filament renders in this container and have to rely on software approximations. I'd love more deterministic city/world planning (like G023 Dove Beach), dialogue/evaluation engineering (like G024), and systemic loop mechanics.

6. **The game:** The single most important missing piece in the playable loop right now is **unified player state + real wallet/inventory ownership in the loop** (plus wiring the already-built G019 court, G020 time-jump, and G021 memories into the phone's `sim.js` and HUD). Right now `LoopSim` returns `payEarned`, `bailPaidCents`, `feeCents`, and `fineAmount`, while expecting the caller to maintain cash, clothing changes, and inventory externally; until those live cleanly in one place and trigger real in-world encounters (walking into the courthouse on court day, stepping into the machine to jump, buying fresh clothes to shed witness descriptions), the loop's pieces feel separate instead of one continuous life. If I chose what to build next, I'd build a compact **Player Life & Wardrobe/Wallet state** in `src/loop/` (cash, owned clothes/disguises, carried items, lodging/rent, and daily bills) wired straight into stops, court fees, and era transitions.

7. **Your ideas:** **Era-mismatched money & objects as quiet time-travel tells.** If the player tries to pay for bread in 1952 with a 2025 $20 bill (or walks around in 2025 wearing a 1920s suit, or pawns a watch whose serial number hasn't been manufactured yet), shopkeepers and pawn clerks shouldn't throw a sci-fi error — they should react like real people ("That bill looks funny, pal — try the bank", "Where'd you get a watch like that?"), feeding directly into `memoriesFor()` and town gossip so a careless time traveller quietly builds a reputation as a strange outsider before the police ever match a fingerprint.

8. **Anything else:** The shift after survey round 1 — especially the self-review questions, boundary conventions in `docs/LOOP_API.md`, and letting us chain clearly independent or explicitly stacked branches (`G020` off `G019`, `G024` off `G021`) while you're away — worked really well. One small thing to watch when merging our stacked branches: `G020` sits cleanly on top of `G019`, and `G024` will sit cleanly on top of `G021`, while `G019/G020` and `G021/G024` only overlap minimally in `src/loop/types.ts` (G021 appended `LoopMemoryEvent` at the very bottom of `src/loop/types.ts`, so any merge with G019/G020 will be a trivial additive merge).
